Why now

If AI writes your UI, your design system is the budget

An agent builds from whatever your system gives it. When components and tokens are reachable, it writes less, reuses more and comes back for fewer corrections. When they are not, it guesses, and you pay for every guess in tokens, review time and rework.

The audit tells you which half of your system an agent can follow, and which half it has to guess. That is the number that decides your AI cost.

The cost of drift

Every value an agent guesses, you pay for twice

Once when it is written, and again when a person finds it in review. Drift is not a tidiness problem; it is a throughput problem.

Tokens

Raw values are long

A component call is one line. The same part written by hand is forty, with every colour, space and state spelled out. The model writes all of it, and you pay for all of it, every time.

Review

Guesses land on a person

A value off the scale looks right and reads wrong. Someone has to catch it, ask for a change, and check the fix. That loop costs more than the generation did.

Consistency

Near-misses ship

Nine greys that are almost the same. A button rebuilt because the real one was not findable. None of it fails a test, and all of it makes the product feel loose.

Accessibility

Conditions slip quietly

Contrast below the minimum, movement that ignores the reduced-motion setting. Nobody notices until someone cannot use the screen.

What the audit checks

Five pillars, one report

Each pillar answers a different question, and each one fails in a different way. The method was tested on four published open-source design systems, Primer, Radix Themes, Carbon and Chakra UI, mature systems with full-time teams behind them. Every one still had findings worth fixing.

  • ValuesEvery colour, space, size, radius, type and motion value in your components, checked against your own tokens: what is hand-written, what equals a token nobody found, and what sits on no scale. Plus the token set itself: what nobody uses, and what is defined twice.
  • PeopleText contrast on every pair your tokens declare, in every theme. Reduced motion on everything that moves. High contrast, visible focus, and right-to-left if you need it.
  • StructureComponents rebuilt by hand where one exists, parts used outside their contract, and styling that overrides what a component is rather than where it sits.
  • ContentNumbers and claims with no source, and the words your voice rules ban. What the system teaches an agent, it repeats in every screen.
  • Agent readinessWhat a coding agent has to invent when it builds with your system: decisions it made up, token names it expected to exist, and decisions that exist but were not findable.

Every finding is checked by hand before it reaches you. You get one report, fixes ranked by cost and gain, and a 30-minute call.

What the audit finds

Four kinds of finding, and only two are mechanical

A percentage tells you little on its own. What matters is which kind of finding you have, because each one costs something different to fix.

Mechanical

A value that equals a token

The decision exists; someone wrote the number instead. Find, replace, and prove with a before-and-after render that nothing moved.

Needs a decision

A value on no scale

An optical nudge, or drift. Only your team can say which. The audit groups them and shows where they cluster, so deciding takes minutes.

Affects people

Contrast and motion

Text colours checked against every background in every theme, and movement checked against the reduced-motion setting. The findings teams act on first.

Getting worse

What AI invents

Give a coding agent your system and it fills the gaps by guessing. The audit measures that too: which decisions it made up, and which existed but were not findable.

The things teams ask before booking

Our system is in Figma, not in code.

Then the audit reads the design file and the product instead, and the drift list compares what is drawn with what ships. Tell me which you have and I will say what the report can cover.

We use Storybook. Does this replace it?

No. Storybook shows that each component works and gives agents your catalogue. The audit measures whether the screens built with it follow the system, and checks the conditions Storybook does not: contrast across themes, reduced motion, high contrast. It runs on top of what you already have.

Can you sign an NDA, or work from an export?

Yes to both. If repository access is a problem, an export of the design system and a few product screens is enough to produce the drift list.

What if the audit finds very little?

That happens, and it is a useful answer: the report says so and names what it checked. The four public systems all scored high on tokens and still had findings worth fixing.

Who does the work?

I do. The scan is automated; the verification, the ranking and the report are mine. Every finding is checked by hand, because automated scans are wrong often enough to matter.
