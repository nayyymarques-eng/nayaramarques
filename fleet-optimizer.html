Fleet Optimizer — Nayara Marques
Loadsmart · Chicago · 2021—2023

Capacity matching for private truck fleets

Fleet Optimizer matches private fleet capacity to shipper demand. A design sprint produced a web platform; a comparison test showed the plain email it would replace was the better bet. We shipped a redesigned email: response rate up 250%.

Role

Senior Product Designer, sole designer. I ran the research and facilitated the sprint.

Team

Me, 2 PMs, and product, business and tech across four countries. Sponsored by The Home Depot.

Context

A new, experimental vertical at a logistics unicorn.

Contribution

qual + quant research · sprint facilitation · concept design · comparison testing · product strategy

01 — Context & my role

Loadsmart automates freight pricing and booking. Fleet Optimizer targeted private and dedicated fleets: companies that own their trucks or serve one shipper.

The team had a concept but no evidence of demand. My job was to find what fleet types shared and take the findings to the PM and VP of Product, including when they pointed away from the plan.

View Matches. The platform the sprint produced and the research shelved.

02 — The problem

Empty return trips are expensive, and nobody was being paid to care.

Four findings from the interviews mattered:

6 of 6

Managers spend most of their time in spreadsheets, chasing fleet metrics.

6 of 6

Dedicated fleets are paid for the round trip. Around 70% of return freight moves empty, and the customer already pays for it.

5 of 6

Dedicated service always comes first. A backhaul that risks the committed route isn't worth it.

6 of 6

Loading time decides it: a live load with strapping and tarping can add four hours to a driver's day.

So backhauls were not the priority we assumed, and capacity depends on shipper volume, hours of service and the load.

03 — Constraints

No fleet manager could join; every output was a hypothesis until tested. Online, with participants in the US, Peru, Brazil and Argentina. Investment depended on evidence. The company could change strategy, and later did.

04 — Process & key decisions

Four stages: interviews, the sprint I facilitated, a comparison test, decisions.

4.1Interviews

Six people from fleet teams, 30 minutes each. They reset the team's priority.

4.2Design sprint

Online, across four countries, with no fleet manager. It produced View Matches, pictured above.

4.3Comparison test

Six participants saw the platform and the current email in turn and picked one. Tactical staff and managers split, which the sprint hadn't modelled.

4.4Key decisions

The sprint's winner was View Matches, a site for uploading capacity and receiving load matches. I tested it against the existing email instead of on its own. We rebuilt the email around the lane and kept View Matches on the roadmap for managers.

05 — The solution

A match email built to be decided on from a phone in a yard.

Before

Led by the fleet number, with price and action below the fold on a phone.

After

Lane first, price and CTA up front, feedback at the end.

Lane, not fleet number

Subject and first line name origin and destination. No record to open.

Price and CTA up front

Accepting is a price decision, so it comes first.

Recurrency stated

One-off load or weekly lane: the email says which.

A way to reply with more than yes

Feedback and add-capacity sections turned a broadcast into a channel, and captured why loads were rejected (mostly loading time).

06 — Impact

6.1The platform

The platform was the wrong surface.

Staff who work the loads chose the email; only managers chose the platform. Recommending the email meant shelving a platform the team had already designed.

What shipped View Matches platform → Redesigned match email

6.2The email channel

The channel was under-used, not exhausted.

Four replies in six months had been read as a dead channel.

+250%

response rate after the redesign

4

replies in the six months before: the baseline

07 — What I learned

"Is this good?" gets agreement. "Which of these two?" gets a decision. A worse portfolio piece, a better product decision. Use the channel users already check. It aligned four countries in a week. Now I write the test for its output before it starts. Days of research moved the plan before any build. Size research to the decision.

08 — What this case doesn't cover

The company later left private fleets: no long-run revenue figure. +250% is a response-rate lift on a small baseline, not a business outcome. Only the matches view is shown; the rest was never tested or built. Six interviews and six test sessions, dedicated fleets only. Enough to redirect the plan, not to generalise. Findings about drivers came from managers. No driver was interviewed.
