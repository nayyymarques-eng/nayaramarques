Where AI lives in an institutional investment platform

AI lived on a separate chat page and treated every question the same way. I moved it into the surfaces people already work in, in two modes: analysis, to ask about what you see, and edit, to change it with review. The patterns now live in the design system.

Role

Senior Product Designer. Interaction design, working prototypes, delivery spec.

Users

Institutional allocators and fund managers: time-poor, sceptical of automation.

Timeframe

2026, ongoing.

Built

In Claude Design on the current design system, handed to engineering through Claude Code.

01 — Context & my role

Allocators and fund managers use the platform for diligence, manager tracking and investment committee prep. Their work is documents, and the decisions carry real money.

AI had arrived as a separate chat page, one click away from that work. I was the designer on the AI surfaces: the audit, placement, behaviour in each place, coded prototypes and the spec engineering built from.

I worked with the product team, not for them. Client conversations showed where AI was worth putting: refining reports generated from the platform's data. Feedback so far is qualitative.

The work is still shipping, so the figures are wireframes from the delivery spec, with invented labels. The composer and its states are a separate case: Composer spec.

02 — The problem

AI was a place you had to go to, and it treated every question the same way.

AI lived on its own chat page. Using it meant leaving the report, and the AI knew nothing about where you came from. People came to ask about what they saw, or to change what they were working on. The chat treated both the same.

2.2Problem B: one behaviour for two situations

Questions arose in two moments: reading, when the page should not change, and working, when the AI changes the output. That change has to be visible, reviewable and reversible before it becomes the record.

Reading, comparing, checking. The answer is information, and the page stays as it is. Rewriting, correcting, restructuring. The answer is a change, so it is reviewed before it is kept.

03 — Constraints

Client feedback set the requirements, not generic AI-chat patterns. Four surfaces risk four features. Every entry point had to behave the same way and share the same parts. Other people act on reports and notes. Any AI change had to be reviewed before it was kept, and recoverable after. The codebase version had drifted, so the work was built on the design system in Claude Design.

AI where the work is, in two modes that behave differently.

5.2Analysis mode

A floating composer over the dashboard, a team view or a report answers questions about what is on screen. It opens attached to the page, changes nothing, and expands into the full chat with its context. Answers arrive with their sources: for this audience, an answer without a source is worse than no answer.

5.3Edit mode

Edit mode opens from the output's toolbar. The agent proposes a change, shown in place against the original; the user accepts it, keeps theirs or asks again. Nothing is saved before review, and every accepted change becomes a version.

Every output type has the same two entry points, so users learn it once.

06 — Mapped for AI

Every pattern here went into the design system, so AI builds the next surface from it instead of inventing one.

Most new screens here start as AI-generated. The floating composer, edit mode and review state became components with rules, so no generated screen draws its own chat box. Four things make that hold; one was missing.

Four surfaces, two modes. A new surface picks a mode, then its component. Analysis attaches context and never edits. Edit shows the change in place and never saves without review. A part the system lacks is drift. After review it becomes a component with rules, or an existing one replaces it. A person checked generated screens by eye. A harness would check every screen against the rules and send a person only what a machine cannot settle.

How the system is written for AI to follow is its own case: Design system. The harness is what I now build for teams: Audit design system.

07 — Impact

The surfaces are rolling out, so claims are structural. Adoption and time-to-answer are being measured, not estimated.

7.1Expected gains

Users ask about a report from inside it, with nothing to re-type. Analysis answers with sources and leaves the page alone. Edit changes the output in place, reviewed before saving. AI reuses the patterns as components, and anything new it draws is reviewed as drift.

08 — What I learned

Where AI belongs comes before anything is drawn. Get it wrong and every later state is wrong too. Asking and changing look alike, but one needs an answer and the other a review. Naming the two modes changed more than any control. Working prototypes removed the ambiguity of static screens. But streaming, agent handoff and versioning are real complexity, and that build belonged to engineering.

09 — What this case doesn't cover

The work is live and unfinished.

Adoption and time-to-answer are still being measured. The counts above are scope, not evidence. Retrieval, accuracy and prompts. This case is the interaction around the model. It drew on the audit, user feedback and product's knowledge of these teams. The research itself is not mine to publish. The split fits the product today, based on feedback and workflow evidence, not tested over time. Specified frame by frame in its own case, Composer spec. Only the AI touchpoints. Diligence, manager tracking and reporting are out of scope.
