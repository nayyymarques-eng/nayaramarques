Where AI lives in an institutional investment platform

AI lived on its own chat page, away from the work, and answered every question the same way. I moved it into the surfaces people already use, and split it into two modes: analysis, for asking about what you see, and edit, for changing what you are working on.

Role

Senior Product Designer. Interaction design, working prototypes, and the delivery spec.

Users

Institutional allocators and fund managers. Sophisticated, time-poor, sceptical of automation.

Timeframe

2026, ongoing. Audit, placement, two modes and prototypes.

Built

Designed in Claude Design on the current design system, and shared with engineering through Claude Code.

01 — Context & my role

The platform serves private capital markets: allocators and fund managers running diligence, tracking managers and preparing for investment committee. Their work is documents (memos, reports, meeting notes), and the decisions that come out of them carry real money.

AI had arrived in the product as a separate chat page, one click away from that work. I was the designer on the AI surfaces: the audit of what existed, where AI should live, how it behaves in each place, the prototypes in code and the spec engineering built from.

It was built with the product team rather than handed to them. Regular conversations about how clients actually work located where AI was worth putting: report editing. With the data already in the platform, a report can be generated more precisely, and the value is in what happens next, editing and refining the generated paper in place. The feedback so far is qualitative; nothing is measured yet.

The work is still shipping, so this case is illustrated with wireframes from the delivery spec rather than product screenshots. Labels are invented; structure and behaviour are as specified. The composer itself, with all its states, is a separate case: Composer spec.

02 — The problem

Two problems. AI was a place you had to go to, and once there it treated every question the same way.

AI lived on its own page: a chat, a composer at the bottom, and nothing else. To use it, people left the report or document they were working on, and the AI knew nothing about where they came from. People came to the chat for two different reasons: to ask about what they were looking at, or to change something they were working on. The chat treated both the same way.
A. AI as a destination

The as-is audit started with where AI lived. It was a page of its own in the navigation: a title, an empty conversation and a composer at the bottom. Nothing on it came from the work the user had just left.

B. Two situations, one behaviour

Watching how questions arose showed two different moments. In the first, the user is reading and wants to understand: summarise this, compare it to last quarter, what changed. Nothing on the page should change. In the second, the user is working on an output and wants the AI to change it: rewrite this action, tighten this section. That moment needs more attention. The change has to be visible, reviewable and reversible before it becomes the record.

Reading, comparing, checking. The answer is information, and the page stays as it is. Rewriting, correcting, restructuring. The answer is a change to the output, so it needs review before it is kept.

03 — Constraints

Product sits closest to the clients, and the clients are a narrow field. Their feedback decided what the experience had to do, ahead of any generic AI-chat pattern. Putting AI in four surfaces risks four different features. Every entry point had to behave the same way and use the same parts, or users would have four things to learn. Reports and meeting notes are what other people act on. Anything the AI changes in them had to be reviewed before it was kept, and recoverable after. The design system lived in Claude Design. The version in the codebase had drifted and its components were inconsistent, so the work was built on the Claude Design system and avoided the outdated ones.

05 — The solution

AI where the work is, in two modes that behave differently.

A. Entry points: from one page to the surfaces

Instead of one page that knows nothing, AI appears in four places: the dashboard, for open questions, and inside reports, documents and meeting notes, each already knowing what it is attached to.

The question a user has about a report is about that report. Asking them to restate it on another page is asking them to describe context the platform already holds.

B. Analysis mode: a composer floating over what you see

A floating composer hovers over the dashboard, a team view or a report. It is for asking about what is on screen: summarise, compare, flag. It opens already attached to the page, and nothing on the page changes. When the conversation grows, it expands into the full chat without losing that context.

C. Edit mode: working on the output with the agent

Edit mode opens from the output's own toolbar. The user chats with the output instead of about it: the agent proposes a change, the change shows in place against the original, and the user accepts it, keeps their version or asks again. Nothing is saved until it is reviewed, and every accepted change is saved as a version.

Edit mode is not a feature of one surface. Every output type carries the same two entry points: an inline edit on the passage itself, and an edit chat docked in the output. The user learns it once.

06 — Mapped for AI

Every pattern here went into the design system, so AI builds the next surface from it instead of inventing one.

Most new screens on the platform start with an AI tool generating them. If the floating composer, edit mode and review only lived in these prototypes, the next generated screen would draw its own chat box, and the product would slowly collect five of them.

So the floating composer, the edit mode and the review state were added to the design system as components, each with rules. Four things make that hold. Three were in place; one was missing.

Four surfaces, two modes. A new surface picks a mode first, then the component that serves it. Analysis attaches context and never edits. Edit shows the change in place and never saves without review. A part the system does not have is drift. It goes to review, then becomes a component with rules or is replaced by one that exists. Nothing verified generated screens against the behaviour rules; a person checked them by eye. A harness would check every screen, flag what breaks a rule, and send a person only what a machine cannot settle.

How the system is written for AI to follow is its own case: Design system. The harness is what I now build for teams: Audit design system.

07 — Impact

The surfaces are rolling out, so what can be claimed today is structural. Adoption and time-to-answer are being measured, and I would rather publish them once than estimate them here.

Where the gain shows up

Edit changes the output in place, with review before it is saved and a version after. The patterns are components with rules, so AI reuses them while it explores, and anything new it draws is reviewed as drift.

08 — What I learned

Where the AI belongs has to be settled before anything else can be drawn, and getting it wrong makes every state after it wrong too. Asking and changing look alike from the outside. They are not: one needs an answer, the other needs a review. Naming the two modes changed more than any control did. Specifying in working prototypes removed the ambiguity a static screen leaves behind. It does not follow that I should have built this one. Streaming, agent handoff and versioning carry real complexity, and that build belonged to engineering.

09 — What this case doesn't cover

The work is live and unfinished. Six things are outside what is shown here.

Adoption and time-to-answer are still being measured. The counts above are scope, what was specified, not evidence that it worked. Retrieval, accuracy and prompt behaviour are not covered. This case is about the interaction around the model, not the model. The argument came from the as-is audit, user feedback and what product knew about how these teams work. The research itself (who was spoken to, how many, what they said) is not mine to publish. Analysis and edit are the right split for what the product does today, decided on feedback and workflow evidence rather than tested over time. More surfaces may change the answer. The composer used by both modes is specified frame by frame in its own case, Composer spec. Only the AI touchpoints are shown. Diligence, manager tracking and reporting are the product this sits inside, and they are not part of this case.
