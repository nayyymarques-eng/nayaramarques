Specifying an AI composer, frame by frame

One bar carried every AI feature in an investment platform, all exposed at once. I respecified it as twenty-six frames in six state families, each with a line an engineer can pass or fail. The same composer serves both AI modes, analysis and edit.

Role

Senior Product Designer. Interaction design, working prototypes, delivery spec.

Users

Institutional allocators and fund managers: time-poor, sceptical of automation.

Timeframe

2026, ongoing.

Built

In Claude Design on the current design system, handed to engineering through Claude Code.

01 — Context & my role

The composer is the way into every AI feature on the platform: one bar on the dashboard, and the same component inside reports, documents and meeting notes. It serves both modes from the AI surfaces case: floating over a page for analysis, and inside an output for edit.

I was the designer on it: the audit, the interaction patterns, coded prototypes and the delivery board engineering built from. The work is still shipping, so the figures are wireframes from that board, with invented labels.

02 — The problem

The first layer offered everything at once, and once you chose something it disappeared into the input.

As-is first layer

My audit found nine controls on the first layer (shortcuts, a shortcuts grid, context, source, research, web, history, workflows, send), all before the user typed. Choosing was harder than asking. And anything selected rendered inside the input, so the question and its context blurred together.

03 — Constraints

No feature could be cut. The composer had to get simpler with everything still reachable.Users need to see what the model sees, but not written into the field they are typing in.The codebase version had drifted, so the work was built on the design system in Claude Design.

04 — The decision

The first layer becomes a field, a plus, a model selector and send. Features move behind two keys users already know: / for commands and shortcuts, + for context. Anything attached becomes a tag row under the input. Twenty-six frames follow from that one move.

05 — The states

Twenty-six frames in six families, each with one line an engineer can pass or fail.

Most states behind the first layer had never been drawn: a failed upload, a picker with nothing to pick, a missing microphone, a model control hidden by a flag.

Where the machine had numbers, the spec uses them: a 120-second recording cap, a countdown at fifteen seconds left, a 44-bar waveform. Seven keyboard bindings are written as a contract. Escape is still open: two panels can be open at once, and only the innermost should close.

06 — Mapped for AI

The spec is written for the next builder, usually an AI tool.

New screens here are usually generated, then refined. A composer that existed only as a picture would be redrawn differently each time. So it lives in the design system as one component, with its states and pass-or-fail lines in its rules.

AI reuses it instead of drawing a new input. Anything the composer lacks is drift: it goes to review, then becomes a new state or is replaced by an existing one.

Floating for analysis, inside the output for edit. Same component, same states, different placement. An engineer, reviewer or AI tool can check a frame against its line without a designer.

How the design system is written for AI to follow is its own case: Design system.

07 — Impact

The composer is in build, so today's claim is the specification itself.

7.1Expected gains

Eight existing parts, each marked remove, move, keep or decide. The two nobody had settled are open questions on paper, not surprises in code. Every edge state has a frame and an acceptance line, so none arrives as a bug report after launch.

7.2Scope of the spec

26composer frames
6state families
8existing parts, each with a disposition

Adoption and time-to-answer are being measured as it rolls out, not estimated.

08 — What I learned

Every setting, and every result of choosing it, needs a line that can pass or fail, written against the stress cases, not the happy path. Drawing the target says nothing about what has to come out. The removal list was what engineering used most, and nobody had asked for it. Working prototypes removed the ambiguity of static screens. But streaming, agent handoff and versioning are real complexity, and that build belonged to engineering.

09 — What this case doesn't cover

Adoption and time-to-answer are still being measured. The counts above are scope, not evidence. Retrieval, accuracy and prompts. This case is the interaction around the model. The brief came from client feedback, through product. That research is not mine to publish. Where AI lives in reports, documents and meeting notes is a separate case: AI surfaces.
