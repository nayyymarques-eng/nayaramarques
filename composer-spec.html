Specifying an AI composer, frame by frame

One bar carried every AI feature in an investment platform and offered everything at once. I respecified it as twenty-six frames across six state families, each with one line an engineer can pass or fail. The same composer serves both AI modes, analysis and edit.

Role

Senior Product Designer. Interaction design, working prototypes, and the delivery spec.

Users

Institutional allocators and fund managers. Sophisticated, time-poor, sceptical of automation.

Timeframe

2026, ongoing. Audit, states and prototypes.

Built

Designed in Claude Design on the current design system, and shared with engineering through Claude Code.

01 — Context & my role

The platform serves private capital markets: allocators and fund managers running diligence, tracking managers and preparing for investment committee. The composer is the way into every AI feature in it: one bar on the dashboard, and the same component inside reports, documents and meeting notes. It serves both modes from the AI surfaces case: floating over a page for analysis, and inside an output for edit.

I was the designer on it: the audit of what existed, the interaction patterns, the prototypes in code, and the delivery board engineering built from. The work is still shipping, so this case is illustrated with the wireframes from that board rather than product screenshots. Labels are invented; structure, states and behaviour are as specified.

02 — The problem

The first layer offered everything at once, and once you chose something it disappeared into the input.

The as-is first layer

I audited the as-is composer and thread. The first layer of the composer carried too much: a shortcuts row and an all-shortcuts grid, a context picker, a source selector, research, web, history, workflows and send, all exposed before the user had typed anything. Choosing was harder than asking. And once something was selected (a document, a team, a generated output), it was rendered inside the composer itself, mixed into the text the user was still writing, so the question and its context were indistinguishable.

03 — Constraints

The brief was not to cut features. Shortcuts, context, source, research, web, history and workflows all had to remain reachable. The composer had to get simpler without losing anything.The user needs to see what the model can see. It could not be shown by writing into the field they are typing in, which is what the as-is did.The design system lived in Claude Design. The version in the codebase had drifted and its components were inconsistent, so the work had to be built on the Claude Design system and avoid the outdated ones.

04 — The decision

The first layer becomes a field, a plus for attaching, a model selector and send. Features move behind two things the user already knows: / for commands and shortcuts, + for context. Everything selected or generated as context then attaches as a tag row under the input. Twenty-six frames follow from that one move, covering the field and send, the attachment bar, the pickers, voice, modes and the model read-out.
What the first layer became

Two moves. The composer's first layer comes down to a prompt, a plus, a model selector and send. Commands live behind / and context behind +. And everything the user has attached moves out of the input into a row of context tags below it, where it stays visible without competing with the question being typed.

05 — The states

The composer was specified as twenty-six frames across six families, each carrying the one line an engineer can pass or fail.

The first layer is a field, a plus, a model read-out and send. Everything behind it has states of its own, and most of them had never been drawn: an attachment that fails to upload, a picker on first run with nothing to pick, a microphone that is not there, a model control that disappears when a flag is off.

Where the machine already had numbers, the spec uses them rather than approximations: a 120-second recording cap, a countdown that appears with fifteen seconds left, a 44-bar waveform. Seven keyboard bindings are written out as a contract, and Escape is still an open question: two panels can be open at once, and only the innermost should close.

06 — Mapped for AI

The spec is written for the next builder, and most of the time that builder is an AI tool.

New screens on the platform are usually generated before a person refines them. A composer described only as a picture would be redrawn from it each time, a little differently. So the composer lives in the design system as one component, with its states and their pass-or-fail lines written into its rules.

When AI builds a new surface, it reuses the composer and its states instead of drawing a new input. When a surface needs something the composer does not have, that is drift: it goes to review as a proposal, and either becomes a new state of the component or is replaced by one that exists.

Floating for analysis, inside the output for edit. Same component, same states, different placement. An engineer, a reviewer or an AI tool can check a frame against its line without asking a designer.

How the design system is written for AI to follow is its own case: Design system.

07 — Impact

The composer is in build, so what can be claimed today is the specification itself.

Where the gain shows up
The spec covers what comes out of the composer as well as what goes in: eight existing parts, each marked remove, move, keep or decide. The two nobody had settled are written down as open questions rather than discovered in code. An upload that fails, a picker with nothing in it, a microphone that is not there: each has a frame and an acceptance line, so none of them arrives as a bug report after launch.

08 — What I learned

Every setting has to be covered, and so does every result of choosing it: the upload that fails, the picker with nothing in it, the microphone that is not there, the control that disappears behind a flag. Each one needs a line that can pass or fail, written against the stress cases rather than the happy path. Drawing what the composer should become says nothing about what has to come out of it. The removal list was the part engineering used most, and it was the part nobody had asked for. Specifying in working prototypes removed the ambiguity a static screen leaves behind. It does not follow that I should have built this one. Streaming, agent handoff and versioning carry real complexity, and that build belonged to engineering.

09 — What this case doesn't cover

The composer is live and unfinished. Four things are outside what is shown here.

Adoption and time-to-answer are still being measured. The counts above are scope, what was specified, not evidence that it worked. Retrieval, accuracy and prompt behaviour are not covered. This case is about the interaction around the model, not the model. What the composer had to do was decided on client feedback reaching me through product. That research itself (who was spoken to, how many, and what they said) is not mine to publish. Where the AI belongs, and how it behaves inside reports, documents and meeting notes, is a separate case: AI surfaces.
