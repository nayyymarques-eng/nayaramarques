Card Insurance — Nayara Marques
BTG Pactual · São Paulo · 2020

Modular card insurance in a banking app

Card insurance came as a fixed bundle at one price. I designed modular cover and assistance inside the bank's app; my usability test then moved the whole flow out of card signup to protect conversion.

Role

Senior Product Designer, sole designer.

Team

PM, tech lead, developers, and Too Seguros as insurer.

Context

BTG+, the bank's digital product. October 2020.

Contribution

survey · benchmark · user flows · prototyping · usability testing · flow strategy

01 — Context & my role

BTG Pactual, one of Latin America's largest investment banks, was expanding into retail banking. Card insurance was one of the new products.

I designed it end to end: survey, benchmark, flows, prototype, and the usability test that changed where it lived.

Note · BTG Pactual

Two and a half years at BTG Pactual: I joined as a consultant on BTG+ and left as Product Design Lead on the advisors platform.

Also the context for An advisors platform for four business verticals.

02 — The problem

Almost everyone already had card insurance. Almost nobody was happy with it.

A survey of 30 BTG Pactual customers:

90%

already have card insurance on at least one card

65%

are unhappy with at least one assistance in their package

80%

would like to modularise their package

The category sells fixed bundles with no visible cost per part. The goal: modules customers pick, each showing its limit and price effect, plus a claim they can follow in the app.

03 — Constraints

A

A third-party insurer. Coverage, pricing and claim rules belonged to Too Seguros. Some claims had to finish outside the app.

B

Regulated language. Terms can't be paraphrased. Transparency had to come from structure, not rewriting.

C

Free and paid products in one checkout. One assistance came free with the plan, which made checkout awkward.

D

Card conversion is sacred. Anything added to card request risked the retail business's key metric.

04 — Process & key decisions

Four stages: principles, benchmark, flows, and the decisions testing produced.

4.1Design principles

Agreed in a workshop with the PM, tech lead and developers. They settled most later arguments.

Simplicity

A fluid process, not an endless shelf of products.

Personalisation

Coverage matched to the customer's life, at a premium they'll pay.

Transparency

No hidden clauses. Conditions explained where the decision is made.

4.2Benchmark

Seven insurers reviewed for contracting, customisation and claims. Three shaped our structure.

  • Hedvig: inviting a friend visibly lowers the monthly price
  • Wrisk: a custom policy built from plain-language questions, one at a time Shaped the structure
  • Pulse: a base module with paid extensions, shared with the household
  • Lemonade: contracting written in plain, friendly language Shaped the structure
  • DeadHappy: underwriting as a short conversation, ending in a stated payout and price
  • Cuvva: sliders raise coverage item by item, with the price updating instantly Shaped the structure
  • Santander: coverage chosen before purchase, with the instalment updating live

4.4Key decisions

Decision 01

Three plans, then one free assistance to choose

Not an open configurator: the customer picks one of three plans, then one assistance module. Personalisation where it matters, three options where it doesn't. Shaped by Cuvva, Wrisk and Lemonade.

Gained

The modularity customers asked for, without pricing every part themselves.

Traded away

True à-la-carte pricing. The 80% get structured choice, not a blank slate.

Decision 02

Design all three flows, not just the sale

Easy cancellation is part of transparency, and the claim is where the product earns or loses trust.

Gained

A product that holds up after purchase, with a clear line where the insurer takes over.

Traded away

Scope. Claims and cancellation took time the business wanted spent on conversion.

Decision 03

Take the insurance out of card signup, because the test said so

I tested both entry points with 10 active customers. The card request risked conversion, mixed free and paid products in one checkout, and was already crowded.

Gained

Insurance became a pending task after card success, with a permanent home in card settings.

Traded away

The highest-intent moment in the funnel, traded to protect card conversion.

05 — The solution

An insurance page in card settings: three plans with limits side by side, assistance modules with detail sheets, a confirmation that restates plan, price and dates before the terms.

Card home. Insurance is reached from settings, not the request flow.

Why: The signup journey was already overloaded, and insurance there put conversion at risk.

Card settings menu. “Seguro do cartão” sits with every other card setting.

Why: One permanent home for contracting, claiming and cancelling.

Plan comparison. Simples, Pleno and Superior side by side, limits stated in the comparison.

Why: Limits next to the price is how transparency works when clauses can't be rewritten.

Assistance selection. Five assistance modules (pet, repairs, home, emergency services, bike), with Too Seguros named below as the insurer.

Why: This is where the package becomes the customer's own, and naming the insurer up front keeps checkout honest.

06 — Impact

Ten active customers tried the prototype from both entry points. Two findings.

6.1Flow placement

Insurance inside the card request put three things at risk (below). Contracting moved to card settings, with a pending task after card success, before any of it was built.

Inside the card request, insurance put three things at risk:

  • Card conversion, the retail business's key metric
  • A checkout mixing the free assistance with paid plans
  • A journey already carrying benefits, Invest+ and the virtual card

07 — What I learned

On the product

Test the entry point, not just the screens

The screens worked in both journeys. Placement was the decision, and only testing the full flow from two starts surfaced it.

On the product

In regulated products, transparency is a layout problem

You can't rewrite the clause, but you can decide what the customer reads before the price.

On the practice

Principles agreed early are cheaper than arguments later

Written with engineering before the first screen, they framed the case for moving the flow in terms everyone had agreed. I still open projects this way.

On the practice

A small test beats a long debate

Ten sessions settled a conversion question that would otherwise have been argued from opinion, or found after launch.

08 — What this case doesn't cover

From the survey to handover for build. Not covered:

01

Post-launch numbers. Attach, claim and cancellation rates. I left before they were measurable.

02

The claim and cancellation screens. Designed, but not shown. The handoff to Too Seguros mid-claim was never tested with customers.

03

Pricing. Set by the insurer. Willingness to pay was surveyed, never tested against real prices; the Pleno and Superior figures above are placeholders.

04

Research depth. Thirty survey responses and ten moderated sessions: enough to settle placement, not to claim anything statistical. No accessibility review was run.
