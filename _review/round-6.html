1. Space around dividers

On phones every sub-section divider now has the same space above and below it, 28px (--space-fluid-xl), less than a section's 36px. Before, the space was 56px on one side and 20 to 28px on the other, so a sub-section on a phone sat further from its content than a section. The space above is measured from the last thing above (a card, a figure, a list's last row), because blocks end differently. Where a list's closing rule sat right above a sub-section's own rule, the list's rule gives way, so there is one line (Benchmark, User flows and Key decisions on card insurance). On desktop two things move on card insurance: Benchmark and User flows, whose headings draw their own rule, now take LAY-09's 56px margin (Benchmark had 36px), and the closing rules of the benchmark list and the user flows give way to the sub-section rule under them (they were two lines 37 and 57px apart).

2. Prototypes keep their desktop shape

The composer mockups (Composer spec, 20 frames; AI surfaces, Analysis mode and Inline edit) keep 720px on phones and scroll sideways inside the figure. 720px is the narrowest width at which no composer bar wraps. Captions stay at the screen's width. Menus that already fit a phone, and the edit chat figure (report above chat on phones, your round 5 verdict), are unchanged.

3. Flows reach the screen edge

Card insurance's user flows and its flow placement figure now scroll to both screen edges on phones; the first step still starts on the gutter, and the rules between flows stay at content width. On phones the flow placement figure gives up its white card so nothing cuts the flows short.
